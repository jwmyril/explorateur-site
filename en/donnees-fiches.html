5. Intended uses, and inappropriate uses

Intended: to situate a territory, compare communes, prepare a diagnosis, feed a report while citing the source and the year.

Inappropriate:

  • treating an absence as a zero;
  • comparing two communes documented in different years without saying so;
  • drawing a conclusion about individuals: this dataset is a territorial aggregate and never descends to the individual.

6. Data quality — what the producer flags

  • The register = what is DECLARED to the ministry, not what is seen on the ground. A school that has closed but was never struck off appears there; a school never surveyed does not.
  • The count uses DISTINCT CIE codes. Whole blocks of pages are physically repeated in some registers; 643 duplicate facility rows were set aside. The two heaviest cases: Artibonite pages 52 to 70 of the PDF (257 rows taken over from pages 47 to 52) and Nord pages 106 to 110 (66 rows from Cap-Haïtien and Limonade reprinted in the middle of Ranquitte). The controle column carries the count commune by commune.
  • Consequence of the previous point: the figure published for Ranquitte (Nord) rests only on the rows that genuinely carry its code. If part of its list was overwritten at printing, the register gives no way to know — we do not fill that hole.
  • The sector comes from the 7th digit of the CIE code, carried by every row, and not from the « SECTEUR PUBLIC / NON PUBLIC » heading, missing or wrong in places (132 rows diverge).
  • page_pdf_debut is the PDF page number, which does not coincide with the number printed at the foot of the page.
  • No personal data (telephone, founder) is published.
  • No departmental value was pushed down to the commune; a commune absent from the register has an empty field, never a zero.
  • 6 entities in the register have no counterpart in the territorial reference (recent communes, or ones the CNIGS layer does not carry): they are published without an atmart_geo_id rather than discarded.
  • Declared and observed do not measure the same thing: the register counts what is inscribed at the ministry, the map counts what a contributor recorded. Their gap describes the state of the information, not the real number of schools.
  • The mapped count adds up school, secondary school and preschool; it leaves out the university, absent from the register.
  • A commune with no declared figure is not a commune without schools: it is missing from the register that was extracted.

7. What this dataset DOES NOT allow you to conclude

Section added by Atmart: it does not exist in the original template, and it is the one no producer fills in spontaneously.

  • The number of schools says nothing about the number of pupils, the quality, or the level taught.
  • The gap between declared and mapped measures the state of the mapping, not the real number of schools.

8. Privacy

No personal data. This dataset is an aggregate by commune; no person is identifiable in it, directly or indirectly, and no disclosure threshold is required. Questions 24 to 27 of the Aether template (consent, sensitive data, impact assessment) are therefore not applicable — and not « unanswered ».

  • A commune missing from the register is not a commune without facilities: it is missing from the register that was extracted.
  • No facility name is published here: the count is enough for the sheet, the named list stays with its producer.

  • No conclusion about access: two communes with five facilities each do not have the same access if one of them is mountainous.

  • drawing a conclusion about individuals: this dataset is a territorial aggregate and never descends to the individual.

6. Data quality — what the producer flags

  • Times under NORMAL CONDITIONS. Road surface, the rainy season, roadblocks, traffic jams and insecurity do NOT enter the calculation. The figure is an optimistic floor, never a travel forecast.
  • A mapped facility is not a working facility: OSM records a building that was seen, not a service that is delivered. « The hospital is 22 minutes away » says nothing about staff, medicines or opening hours.
  • OpenStreetMap is contributory: a lightly mapped commune has few roads AND few destinations, which lengthens its times or makes them incalculable. That is a defect of the map, not of the territory.
  • No sea link enters the calculation. Island communes (La Tortue, Île à Vache, La Gonâve) have no time to the mainland — and must not have one.
  • One-way streets are ignored and the last stretch to the door is not counted: two further biases, both on the optimistic side.
  • The « hospital » family follows OSM tagging, which in Haiti files many dispensaries and health centres under that word. It is broader than the word suggests.
  • The coordinates of the markets tracked by WFP are published to the hundredth of a degree, that is about one kilometre of precision.
  • The scale lists fast road classes (motorway, expressway) that exist nowhere in Haiti: the highest speed actually applied is that of primary roads.
