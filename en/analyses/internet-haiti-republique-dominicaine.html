Fewer than one person in two uses the Internet in Haiti, against nine in ten in the Dominican Republic

By Atmart Data · 2026-09-18 · Haiti in the Caribbean

In 2024, 47.9% of Haiti's population used the Internet in the previous three months, according to the International Telecommunication Union as reported by the World Bank. In the Dominican Republic, the share is 91%.

The gap is not the result of stagnation: in Haiti, the share has been multiplied by 3.4 since 2015 (14.2%). But the Dominican Republic started higher (54.2%) and grew as well.

Of the 15 Caribbean countries compared, Haiti has the lowest share; the next country, Saint Lucia, is at 68.2%.

The figure counts people who went online at least once in three months, whatever the device or the quality of the connection. Depending on the country and year, the value comes from a survey or from an ITU estimate.

The group compares the sixteen independent Caribbean countries, three of them on the mainland (Belize, Guyana, Suriname); Puerto Rico and dependent territories are not included.
