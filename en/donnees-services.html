Fund continuity, not closed data

Services and partnerships

The Haiti Explorer is free, account-free and tracker-free, and will stay that way. What is charged here is not access to the data: it is the work that makes it reliable, documented and usable — and the guarantee that it will still exist next year.

A rule that is not negotiable. No engagement makes public data less accessible. What one client funds benefits everyone: a gap filled for them is filled for all, and published the same day.

Pillar 1

Sustainable funding and services

Fund the continuity of the public good without turning the data into a closed product.

Sponsorships

Open a dataset, adopt a vintage, fill a gap, maintain a layer. The sponsor is named on the dataset sheet, with the date — and gains no exclusivity: the data is public from the day it is published.

What makes this measurable: the map « Where our information is weakest » names the communes where a sponsorship truly changes something. Today, 8 communes have two data families out of three in disagreement or unverifiable.

Engagements

Territorial diagnosis, quality assurance, reference harmonisation, accessibility, risk analysis, siting studies, dashboards.

What sets this work apart: every deliverable states what it does not allow you to conclude. A report with no « limits » section is not a short report, it is an incomplete one.

Atmart Verified

Audit of a dataset: provenance, quality, geocoding, internal consistency, consent, licence and documentation. The deliverable is a sheet following « Datasheets for Datasets » (Gebru et al.), Aether version, adapted to territorial data.

What exists nowhere else: completeness is recounted from the file at every generation, never copied from a note. And four sections are added to the original model — measured completeness, agreement between sources, legal passport, and « what this dataset does not allow you to conclude », the one no producer fills in spontaneously.

The diagnostic sheets

That last section is the one you find nowhere else; it is the same rule as for our data: a deliverable that hides its limits is not a short report, it is an incomplete one.

🔍 Atmart Verified — audit of a dataset

Who it is for

Data producers, NGOs, ministries, projects about to publish or to fund a collection.

Lead time

Two to three weeks per dataset.

Price

On request — the scope decides the cost

The questions this diagnostic settles

  • Does this dataset really cover the territory it claims to cover?
  • Do its figures agree with the other public sources?
  • Do we have the right to publish it, and to charge for it?
  • What does it NOT allow you to conclude?

What you receive

A documentation sheet following the « Datasheets for Datasets » model (Gebru et al., Aether version), with four sections added by Atmart: completeness measured on the file, agreement between sources, a legal passport with two verdicts, and limits of interpretation.

An example already produced, and public

Example already delivered: Six sheets already produced and public — declared schools, declared health, concordance, road access, population from three sources, OpenStreetMap facilities.

What the Explorer adds, and that a lone provider does not have

the 38 legal passports and the six sheets already published serve as model and yardstick — the audit does not start from a blank page, it starts from a method already tested in public, which the client can inspect before signing.

See the published proof

What this diagnostic does NOT do

  • It does NOT correct the dataset: it describes it. Correction is a separate engagement.
  • It does not grade the TERRITORY, only the information we have about it.
  • It does not replace legal advice: it establishes what the producer's terms say, and archives the proof.

🧭 Territorial diagnosis

Who it is for

Local authorities, funders, NGOs that must choose WHERE to intervene and justify that choice.

Lead time

Three to six weeks depending on the number of dimensions.

Price

On request — the scope decides the cost

The questions this diagnostic settles

  • Which communes accumulate fragilities?
  • Where is the information so weak that no decision is defensible without collecting data first?
  • Which communes resemble each other enough for a scheme to be replicated there?

What you receive

A report with maps, a reproducible ranking table, and the method written out so that anyone can redo the calculation. The intermediate files are delivered, not only the conclusions.

An example already produced, and public

Example already delivered: The map « Where our information is weakest » published on the Explorer: 8 communes have two data families out of three in disagreement or unverifiable.

What the Explorer adds, and that a lone provider does not have

the 140 communes are already described, cross-checked and dated: the diagnosis begins where a lone provider would just be finishing their collection. And the concordance index says in advance where the information is too weak to decide on.

See the published proof

What this diagnostic does NOT do

  • It does not produce field data: it works from what exists and says where what exists is not enough.
  • It does not rank communes by « merit » — a ranking measures the state of the INFORMATION as much as that of the territory.

🔗 Reference harmonisation

Who it is for

Organisations whose several databases speak of the same territories without agreeing: different names, missing codes, diverging boundaries.

Lead time

Two to four weeks depending on volume.

Price

On request — the scope decides the cost

The questions this diagnostic settles

  • How many of our records really match an identified commune?
  • Where do our databases contradict each other?
  • How do we keep the alignment when the sources change?

What you receive

A crosswalk table to the official p-codes, the exact count of what could not be matched — and why — and a reproducible script your teams can rerun on their own.

An example already produced, and public

Example already delivered: The Explorer's territorial reference: 140 communes, 570 sections, stable identifiers, matching through a parent tree.

What the Explorer adds, and that a lone provider does not have

the reference set of 11,707 entities and its parent tree already exist, public and versioned: your correspondence table aims at a stable target, not at a file that will move without warning.

See the published proof

What this diagnostic does NOT do

  • We never match an ambiguous record to the « nearest » one. What does not match stays UNMATCHED, counted and listed — an invented correspondence cannot be detected afterwards.

Pillar 2

Partnerships, capacity and recognition

Build an ecosystem of producers, users, maintainers and funders that reduces dependence on Atmart alone. A public good that rests on one company is not a public good: it is a service with a breaking point.

  1. The annual consortium of Explorer Guardians

    Members who fund the core and sit on its governance. They do not decide the figures — the methods stay public and contestable — they decide the completeness priorities.

  2. Agreements with producers

    Public producers, universities, libraries, diaspora networks, sector organisations. The purpose of an agreement is not to obtain a file: it is to make a divergence repairable, on both sides.

  3. University labs

    Internships, reproduction of indicators, validation, translation and documentation. An indicator a student cannot reproduce is not an indicator, it is a claim.

  4. Digital Public Good application

    Starting from a gap analysis, then budgeting the annual review. We begin by measuring what is missing against the nine criteria — not by filing an application.

  5. Infrastructure programmes

    Apply only after eligibility is confirmed, and with a vendor exit plan written in advance. A dependency taken on without an exit is a permanent dependency.

  6. Caribbean replication

    To be assessed after the Haitian core proves sustainable. Expanding before being sustainable multiplies a fragility.

What is not for sale, and never will be

  • Access to the Explorer's public data — free, no account, no advertising tracker.
  • Exclusivity on a dataset: a sponsorship funds a publication, it never reserves its use.
  • Data whose licence forbids commercial use — two sources in the registry are in this case and are refused for engagements.
  • A figure without its source, its vintage and its limit.
  • An individual observation: everything is aggregated by commune, no person is identifiable.
