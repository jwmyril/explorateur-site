In short

Density divides a commune's estimated population by its official area: it says how many people live, on average, on each square kilometre. In 2024, it ranges from 85.1 inhab./km² in Pointe à Raquette to 27,087 inhab./km² in Port-au-Prince. Half of the communes have fewer than 347.4 inhab./km², the other half more. Gressier, whose population estimate is contradicted by the two other sources, is left out of these extremes.

Such a gap reflects the size of communes as much as their population. A small urban commune quickly reaches very high densities; a large mountain commune spreads its people over nearly empty slopes. Density helps anticipate pressure on land, water, schools and networks; it does not say where, within the commune, people live.

What this indicator does not allow you to conclude

This density does not show that a commune is overcrowded or lacks services, and it does not locate dense neighbourhoods: it is an average over the whole area, mountains and uninhabitable land included.

An estimated population divided by an official area yields an estimated density (status E), never a count. The area includes uninhabitable land: in mountainous and coastal communes, lived density exceeds calculated density. Because the national total of the polygons (26,942 km²) falls short of the usual 27,750 km², every value is very slightly raised. Known source anomaly: COD-PS 2024 assigns 3,987 inhabitants to Gressier (HT0116), out of proportion with its neighbours; we publish the value unchanged, and Gressier's density should not be read.

Indicator sheet

Definition
Estimated resident population per square kilometre of the territorial entity.
Method
IND-POP-001 ÷ IND-GEO-001: the 2024 projected population divided by the area of the 2018 CNIGS boundaries. The two reference years differ — this is assumed, not hidden: the boundaries did not change between them.
How to read it
High = pressure on land, services and networks. Read it alongside the area: the same density does not mean the same thing over 21 km² and over 490 km².
Comparability
Comparable across all 140 communes of the 2018 CNIGS reference: numerator and denominator both cover the whole country, with no blind spot.
