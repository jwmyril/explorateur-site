Seven in ten people in Haiti lack basic sanitation

By Atmart Data · 2026-09-18 · Haiti in the Caribbean

In 2024, 29.1% of Haiti's population has basic sanitation: improved toilets that are not shared with other households. This is the definition of the WHO-UNICEF Joint Programme, used by the World Bank.

In the 10 other Caribbean countries that report this figure for 2024, the share ranges from 84.3% (Belize) to 99.6% (Antigua and Barbuda). The Dominican Republic is at 90.1%.

Progress is slow: 27% in 2015, 29.1% in 2024, a gain of 2.1 points in 9 years.

Lacking basic sanitation does not mean having nothing: the category covers shared toilets, unimproved facilities and no toilet at all. Between two surveys, values are estimates.

The group compares the sixteen independent Caribbean countries, three of them on the mainland (Belize, Guyana, Suriname); Puerto Rico and dependent territories are not included.
