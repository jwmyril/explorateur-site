In short

The European Space Agency's satellites classify every 10-metre plot by what covers it. In 2021, the share classified as “trees” ranges from 0.3 % in Desdunes to 90.2 % in Chambellan; in half of the communes, trees cover less than 40.6 % of the territory.

This figure often surprises those who know the national forest area, which is much lower. The two do not measure the same thing: the satellite counts everything that carries trees — orchards, hedges, Creole gardens, dense bush — whereas forest statistics only keep forest in the strict sense.

What this indicator does not allow you to conclude

This share does not show that a commune is forested in the forestry sense, nor does it measure deforestation: it is a snapshot of a single year, and an orchard or a hedge counts as trees.

Tree cover in the satellite's sense is not forest in the forestry sense: orchards, hedgerows and dense scrub are included. Compare it with the World Bank's national forest area, which gives an entirely different figure — two definitions, two methods.
