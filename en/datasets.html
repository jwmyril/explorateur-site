Haiti and the Dominican Republic — sixteen identical questions

THE EXPLORATEUR'S FIRST CARIBBEAN COMPARISON, and it covers the two countries of one island. Sixteen identical questions in 2023, eleven established gaps. FOUR STRIKING RESULTS: the intention to emigrate separates the two countries by 39 points (78.5 against 39.7); trust in the head of government separates them by 39 points THE OTHER WAY (13.0 against 51.8); Haitians trust their police MORE than Dominicans trust theirs (58.6 against 32.8); and they are asked for bribes eight times less often (2.4 against 18.8). Crime victimisation, however, is almost identical (25.4 against 24.2) and the gap is NOT established. THE WARNING GOVERNS EVERY READING: Haiti was surveyed BY TELEPHONE, the Dominican Republic FACE TO FACE. A gap therefore mixes a difference between countries with a difference between instruments — people do not say the same thing to an interviewer at their door as to a voice on the phone. This product shows where to look, not what to conclude.

THE ATLAS MEASURED INSECURITY BY EVENTS, never by what people live through — and it had NOTHING on governance or justice. Households living on remittances — by department

THE TWO MEASURES DO NOT GIVE THE SAME RANKING, AND YOU NEED TO KNOW WHY BEFORE CONCLUDING ANYTHING FROM IT. Grand'Anse receives 0.7 % of the amounts but 4.5 % of its households have remittances as their main income source; Sud receives three and a half times more money per head and counts only 0.5 %. THESE GAPS PROBABLY DO NOT MEASURE DEPENDENCE: the central bank records the money WHERE IT IS PAID OUT, and payout network density tracks per-capita amounts at +0.79 across departments — the official map of the money is first a map of the teller windows. Excluding Port-au-Prince, amounts and dependence are no longer correlated at all (−0.27). On top of that, the FAO does not separate remittances from abroad from those sent within Haiti. Ten departments, three survey rounds from June 2023 to June 2024, three ranks of income source. WHAT THIS FIGURE IS NOT: the share of households that RECEIVE a remittance, which would be far higher. Forced returns to Haiti — entry points and profile

270,214 people were forcibly returned to Haiti in 2025, 36% more than in 2024, including 265,215 from the Dominican Republic (98%).
