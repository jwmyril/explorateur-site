In 2023, 67% of Haitians would find a military coup justified when crime is high

By Atmart Data · September 19, 2026

The question is hypothetical: "would a military coup be justified if there were a lot of crime?" In 2023, 67% of Haitians surveyed said yes. The figure was 19% in 2012, 29% in 2014 and 29% in 2017.

The metropolitan area stands apart: 47% in 2023, against 70% to 75% in the four other regions. It is the only regional gap the margins of error allow us to assert.

In the Dominican Republic, the same year, 35% said yes.

Two cautions. The 2023 survey was conducted by phone, the earlier ones face to face: the jump since 2017 mixes a change of opinion with a change of method. And the question was put to only part of the sample (1,078 people). It measures a disposition, not support for an actual plan.

Support for democracy fell 13 points between 2006 and 2017, before the switch to phone surveys

By Atmart Data · September 19, 2026

The survey asks whether people agree that democracy may have problems but is better than any other form of government. On a scale converted to 0-100, Haitians stood at 71 in 2006 and at 58 in 2017.

Both waves were conducted the same way, face to face, so the decline is not a method effect. The 2021 and 2023 phone surveys give 49 and 52, but they cannot be compared directly with the earlier ones.

In the Dominican Republic, the score goes from 75 in 2008 to 65 in 2023.

A score of 50 does not mean half the people reject democracy: it is the average of a 1-to-7 scale whose midpoint, 4, equals 50. Results by region are on the "What Haitians think" page.
