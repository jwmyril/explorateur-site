In 2006, blank and spoiled ballots ranged from 4.1% to 31.5% depending on the commune

By Atmart Data · September 18, 2026

In the first round of the 7 February 2006 presidential election, the share of blank and spoiled ballots ranges from 4.1% in Delmas to 31.5% in Beaumont, across the 107 communes whose summary was kept. The median commune is at 16.4%.

It exceeds 20% in 34 communes and stays below 10% in 26. From one department to another, the median ranges from 9.8% (Artibonite) to 21.4% (Centre).

Blank ballots were at the heart of the 2006 dispute: at national level, the CEP later distributed them among the candidates. These figures are those of the commune summaries, BEFORE that distribution.

A blank ballot is a choice, a spoiled one may be a filling error: both are added here. The summaries were printed on 20 February, sometimes before counting ended; the archive kept nothing for the Nord and Sud-Est.

In 2015, 39 of 66 mayors were elected with less than a quarter of the vote

By Atmart Data · September 18, 2026

In the municipal elections of 25 October 2015, the slate elected to the town hall won less than 25% of valid votes in 39 of the 66 communes whose final results the CEP published, and less than a third in 51. None reached half the vote.

The score ranges from 7.59% in Cité Soleil to 45.54% in Dame Marie; half of the winning slates scored below 23.2%.

The election was decided in a single round: the list that came first won, whatever its score. When lists are numerous, a mayor can be elected by a small minority of voters.

The score measures how dispersed the vote was, not the legitimacy or the work of the elected team. The CEP published only 66 communes out of 140; the pages are scans keyed in by hand by Atmart, each checked against its own printed percentage.
